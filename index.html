Who We Are

Protecting a National Treasure

Southern Conservation Partners is assisting individuals and allied organizations who wish to collaborate to achieve the long-term protection, stewardship, and integrity of North Carolina's extraordinary Black River and its primary tributary streams. A major tributary to the Cape Fear River, the Black River's ecological and historical heritage includes swamp forests that are home to ancient bald cypress trees — 1,000 to nearly 3,000 years old.


Friends of the Black River NC is in the process of formally incorporating as an independent organization, for which Southern Conservation Partners serves as fiscal sponsor and collaborator.

Our Mission

A High-Quality Blackwater Stream & Ancient Floodplain Forests

The Friends of the Black River (NC) is dedicated to interpreting and championing natural and historical resources along the river to foster public understanding of its unique environment and provide memorable experiences for visitors.

We believe positive economic impacts accrue for the Wilmington area and southeastern North Carolina when natural and cultural heritage tourism is promoted in the Lower Cape Fear region. Working together, we can secure the future of this natural treasure and benefit generations to come.

Our Goals

  1. Create an association of individuals, organizations, businesses and public partners to champion long-term protection of the entire Black River corridor.
  2. Expand public recognition of the exceptional natural assets of the Black River, particularly its extensive old-growth swamp forests containing the oldest trees in eastern North America.
  3. Defend the outstanding water quality of the river and its tributary streams against threats.
  4. Recognize and reward private landowners for good conservation stewardship of land, water, and natural resources.
  5. Explore options for compatible conservation actions safeguarding traditional private land ownership and stewardship.

Mission: To ensure long-term protection, security, and care for the nation's largest expanse of old-growth swamp forest and other natural communities, biota, and historic features of North Carolina's Black River and its tributary streams.
